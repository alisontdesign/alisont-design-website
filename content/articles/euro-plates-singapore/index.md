'Euro-style' plate — black lettering on white at the front, black on reflective yellow at the rear — has become one of the most requested looks in Singapore. And unlike most styling trends, this one has something rare going for it: **it is exactly what the Singapore authorities themselves prescribe** for reflective plates.

## What the rule actually says

LTA's [standard registration requirements](https://onemotoring.lta.gov.sg/content/onemotoring/home/buying/vehicle-types-and-registrations/car/standard_registration.html) set out two legal colour schemes for ordinary vehicles. The default: "The vehicle registration number should be in white lettering on a black background." That is the classic black plate you still see on older cars.

But there is a second, modern option: "However, if the vehicle licence plate is made of a reflective material" the scheme flips — "For the front, the licence plate should have black lettering on a white background" and "For the back, the licence plate should have black lettering on a yellow background".

Read that again: the moment your plate is reflective material, black-on-white front and black-on-yellow rear is not a European import or a grey-area modification. It is **the** prescribed look.

## What 'Euro-style' must still obey

Colour scheme is only one requirement. A European-look plate is only legal if it also keeps:

- **Character dimensions** — "Letters and digits are 70 mm high, 50 mm wide, and 10 mm broad", and "The space between adjoining letters or between adjoining digits is 10 mm". Squishing the characters to fit a slimmer plate is how styling becomes an offence.
- **Correct placement** — "The vehicle licence plate must be displayed on the front and back of your vehicle, with the vehicle number clearly shown."
- **A genuine flat rectangular plate** — the law requires the registration to "be exhibited on a flat rectangular plate" (Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Registration and Licensing) Rules, rule 17). Curved, recessed or angled mounts create reading errors — for cameras and for people.

In short: any shop can paint a yellow rectangle. A legal Euro-style plate is the prescribed colours, the prescribed character geometry, and a flat, clearly displayed plate. If a quote sounds cheaper because it skips the geometry, that is what you are paying less for.

## The honest limitation

The rules are colour- and dimension-specific, and neither LTA nor the statute names any styling term — 'Euro' is a marketing word, not a legal category. That cuts both ways: it protects your plate choice when done to spec, and it means anyone claiming 'any colour goes as long as it's reflective' is inventing rules. The scheme is fixed: white front, yellow rear, black characters — nothing else.
