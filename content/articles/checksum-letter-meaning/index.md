If you have ever looked at a Singapore number plate and wondered why the last character is a letter — and why it seems random — you are not alone. That final letter is not decorative. It is a **checksum**: a built-in error detector that tells the authorities (and you) whether the rest of the plate was written or read correctly.

## The idea in one sentence

A checksum letter is computed from everything in front of it, so a plate whose last letter does not match the rest of the characters is, almost certainly, misread, mistyped — or tampered with.

This is why traffic cameras, ERP systems and police reports can catch a wrongly transcribed plate number instantly: an altered or misread digit almost always breaks the checksum.

## How the letter is calculated

Singapore uses a weighted-sum scheme. According to [Wikipedia's description of Singapore plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Singapore), the calculation starts by "converting the letters into numbers, i.e., where A=1 and Z=26". Only part of the prefix is used: "For a three-letter prefix, only the last two letters are used", a two-letter prefix uses both, and a single-letter prefix sits in the second position with the first treated as zero. "For numerals less than four digits, additional zeroes are added in front as placeholders".

Then the arithmetic: "Each individual number is then multiplied by 6 fixed numbers (9, 4, 5, 4, 3, 2). These are added up, then divided by 19." The remainder of that division maps to a letter from a fixed set of 19 letters — and the published mapping is famously quirky: it starts 'A' for a remainder of 0, then continues Z, Y, X, U, T, S, R, P, M, L, K, J, H, G, E, D, C, B.

Two details people notice once they know the system: "The letters F, I, N, O, Q, V and W are not used as checksum letters" — so if your plate ends in one of those, it is not a checksum plate. And "Checksum suffix letters are not applied to special government vehicles and event vehicles", which is why some plates you see on the road follow no visible pattern at all.
