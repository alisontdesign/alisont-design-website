## What the law regulates

The Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Registration and Licensing) Rules say your registration number must be "exhibited on a flat rectangular plate or on a flat unbroken rectangular surface forming part of the motor vehicle" and that the plate "must at all times be securely fastened to the motor vehicle" [3]. Note what the law does *not* say: it never specifies metal, gel, acrylic or a printing method. A 3D-printed acrylic plate is legal if it meets the format rules below.

## The five rules your plate must pass

1. **Exact character dimensions.** "All letters and figures shall be 70 mm high, every part of every letter and figure shall be 10 mm broad and the total width of the space taken by every letter or figure, except in the case of the figure one, shall be 50 mm" [3]. Spacing: 10 mm between characters, margins of at least 10 mm top/bottom and 20 mm at the sides [3].
2. **Correct position.** The mark must be "exhibited at the front and at the back of the motor vehicle not more than one metre from the ground in a vertical position" and easily distinguishable [3]. A crooked plate in a deep frame that hides characters fails this.
3. **Nothing near the number.** "No other figures or letters and no design advertisement or ornamentation shall be placed near the identification mark in such a manner as to be liable to render it more difficult to read or distinguish" [3]. That rules out cluttered sticker frames and sponsor plates.
4. **No tampering with the display.** You must not alter the plate so that characters become "less easily distinguishable", so that they "become or act as retroreflective characters", or so as to "prevent or impair the making of a true and clear photographic or recorded image" of the number [3]. In plain English: no smoked covers, no reflective sprays, no angled "number-plate-flip" tricks.
5. **Keep the seals intact.** "It shall be an offence for anyone, except a person authorised by the Registrar, to remove, break or tamper with a seal affixed under these Rules" [3]. On sealed schemes (ROPC, classic, vintage), your plate supplier must leave the old seals alone and resealing happens only at inspection centres [9].

Rear plates must also stay "adequately illuminated" between sunset and sunrise so every character stays readable [3].

## The one-line test

If an enforcement officer can read your number easily, from the correct distance and angle, in the official colours and sizes, with seals intact — you're compliant [3]. Anything added "for style" that works against any of that is where plates become illegal.
