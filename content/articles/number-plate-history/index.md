Singapore's number plates carry more history than most drivers realise. The system on your bumper today is the latest version of a 120-year-old scheme — and it nearly looked completely different.

## 1896: the first automobile

The automobile arrived in Singapore in August 1896, when the Katz Brothers imported the first motor vehicles, selling Benz "horseless carriages" to the colonial elite [6]. For ten years there were simply no number plates at all — there was barely anyone to regulate.

## 1906: S-1, the first registered car

Car registration came into force in 1906, and Singapore's first registered car — a Star motorcar owned by Mrs G.M. Dare, nicknamed "Ichiban" ("Number One") — bore the plate S-1 [6]. That single-letter "S" prefix for Singapore came from the Straits Settlements system, in which S was assigned to Singapore and the basic "S ####" format ran for decades before expanding to "Sx ####" after rationalisation in 1948 [7].

## The reserved letters that started it all

Even in those earliest days, letters were reserved by vehicle class: S'B to S'Y were issued to cars, while S'A was reserved for motorcycles, S'H for taxis, S'D for municipal vehicles and S'G for goods vehicles [2]. The modern class-letter system (G for goods, Y/X for heavy goods, taxis in red) is a direct descendant of that colonial-era logic [2].

## 1965 and 1972: going our own way

After separation in 1965, the S series passed from Malaysian administration to the State of Singapore Registry of Vehicles and its successor, the Land Transport Authority [7]. When the car series ran out at SY in January 1972, private cars switched to the E prefix — E was chosen precisely because letters A to D were already in use by Malaysian states [2]. The E series (EA to EZ) ran from 1972 to 1984 [2].

## 1971: the colour revolution

On 1 August 1971 a new colour scheme took effect, announced in May 1971: white lettering on black at the front, reflective yellow lettering on black at the rear, and vehicles registered earlier had to re-plate [2]. In 1984 the rear plate became the reflective black-on-yellow we know today [2].

## 1984: the modern format is born

From 1984 the S series returned — now as three letters, starting at SBA — and it hasn't stopped since [2]. Some prefixes were skipped and reserved: SBS and SMB, for example, are held back for public bus operators SBS Transit and SMRT Buses [2]. The SG prefix, once reserved for goods vehicles, was revived in November 2015 for government-owned buses under the Bus Contracting Model [2].

## The checksum letter

Along the way Singapore added its distinctive checksum letter: older plates like SG5999 were re-issued with a suffix, becoming SG5999Z [2].
