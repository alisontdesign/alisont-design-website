A blown number plate bulb is invisible to you — the light shines at your own rear plate, where you never look. Yet it is one of the easiest things to be marked for at inspection, because the requirement is old, simple, and applies every single night.

## The rule, word for word

Singapore's Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Registration and Licensing) Rules deal with plate illumination in rule 21. The core duty: "Whenever a motor vehicle is on a road between sunset and sunrise, the identification mark exhibited at the back of the motor vehicle" "shall be adequately illuminated as to render easily distinguishable every letter and figure of the identification mark."

There is one carve-out: "It shall not be necessary for a motor vehicle to comply with paragraph (1) while it is parked on an adequately lighted road."

Three practical readings of that text:

1. **The duty is nightly, not occasional.** Between sunset and sunrise, on any road. Not just highways, not just when it rains.
2. **The standard is functional, not technical.** The law says the characters must be *easily distinguishable*. A faint bulb that barely glints off the plate does not obviously meet that.
3. **It targets the rear plate.** The rule text speaks of the mark "exhibited at the back of the motor vehicle" — that is where the illumination duty sits.

## Are LED plate bulbs legal?

The rule specifies brightness of outcome, not bulb chemistry — so the moment you read it, the answer about LED replacements follows: nothing in rule 21 cares *how* the plate is lit, only that it is lit adequately. Swapping a yellowed filament bulb for a proper white LED that lights the plate evenly changes nothing the law objects to — it makes the plate *more* legible, which is the entire point of the rule.

What the law does object to is lights aimed at defeating it. The same Rules prohibit altering the plate or its display so as to "prevent or impair the making of a true and clear photographic or recorded image" of the mark, and separately ban making the characters do things they should not — the rules specifically cover attempts to "become or act as retroreflective characters". In plain terms:

- A clean white bulb or LED that illuminates the plate: exactly what the rule wants.
- Tinted, coloured, flashing or strobing 'plasma' plate lights, or anything designed to glare out a camera flash: squarely in the territory the tampering provisions were written for.

And illumination is only the night-time half of legibility. Anything mounted near the plate — bold frames, badges, ornaments — runs into rule 19: "No other figures or letters and no design advertisement or ornamentation shall be placed near the identification mark" in a way that makes it harder to read. ## A 30-second weekly check

Walk behind your car after dark and look at the rear plate with your lights on. Can you read every character easily from a car length away? If the light is dim, pinkish or flickering, the bulb is on its way out — and unlike headlamps, a plate lamp failure is the kind of quiet non-compliance that surfaces at the most inconvenient moment.
