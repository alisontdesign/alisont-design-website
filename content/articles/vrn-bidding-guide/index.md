Want S1, S888, or your lucky number on a plate? Singapore sells desirable registration numbers (VRNs) by public auction. Here is exactly how the exercise works, step by step, from the official LTA rules.

## What is a VRN, and do you have to bid?

Every vehicle in Singapore must have a Vehicle Registration Number (VRN) displayed on the front and back plates [4]. When you register a new vehicle, LTA assigns one from the current pool free of charge — there is no charge for a VRN automatically assigned by LTA [4]. You only need to bid if you want a number of your choice [4].

## Step 1 — Check availability and wait for the exercise

LTA runs main bidding exercises for whole new prefixes plus weekly exercises for released numbers [4]. Each exercise has a fixed window: for example, the October 2026 exercise for the SPN and FCA series opened at 1.00am on Friday 23 October and closed at 4.30pm on Wednesday 28 October 2026 [5]. You can bid online on the OneMotoring website throughout the window [5].

## Step 2 — Submit your bid

The rules are simple and fixed [5]:

- Minimum bid: $1,000 (inclusive of GST); anything higher must be in multiples of $1 [5].
- One submission lets you list up to 3 different numbers of the same prefix at the same bid amount, in order of preference [4][5].
- A non-refundable service fee of $18.53 (inclusive of GST) is charged per bid submission via eNETS Debit [5].
- Payment up to $100,000 is by eNETS Debit; above $100,000 you may use a Cashier's Order [5].

## Step 3 — Results: win one, pay your price, refund if you lose

If your bid is successful, only 1 VRN is awarded, based on your order of preference, and the price you pay is exactly the amount you bid [4]. If your bid is unsuccessful, your bid amount will be refunded [4]. This is a sealed, one-shot format — no proxy bidding wars on screen, but no second-chance sniping either.

## Step 4 — Use the VRN before your car is registered

Timing matters. You must bid for your preferred VRN before registering your new vehicle [4]. If you don't, LTA auto-assigns a number when your dealer registers the car — and replacing an auto-assigned VRN costs $327 (inclusive of GST) [4]. You can also retain the VRN of your existing vehicle and assign it to your new one instead of bidding [4].

Remember: if the car is registered under another person's or company's name, a VRN bid under your name cannot be used on that vehicle [4].

## What do winning numbers cost?

Minimum is $1,000 — but lucky-number fever is real. The record public sale cited for a single plate is S$335,000, paid for S32H [2]. Numbers with 8s and 9s and short serials routinely sell far above reserve.
