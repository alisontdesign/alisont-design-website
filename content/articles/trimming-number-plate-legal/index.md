## What actually decides if a trimmed plate is legal

A trim lives or dies on one thing: **whether the number itself still meets the prescribed format.** The Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Registration and Licensing) Rules say what must remain true on the plate — not whether you cut it.

1. **Characters must stay 70 mm tall.** "All letters and figures shall be 70 mm high, every part of every letter and figure shall be 10 mm broad and the total width of the space taken by every letter or figure, except in the case of the figure one, shall be 50 mm" [3]. A trim that scales the whole plate down — characters and all — fails on day one.
2. **The margins must survive.** "The space between adjoining letters and between adjoining figures shall be 10 mm and there shall be a margin between the nearest part of any letter or figure and the top or bottom of the black surface upon which the identification mark is inscribed of at least 10 mm and between the nearest part of any letter or figure and the sides of the black surface of at least 20 mm" [3]. If your cut line runs through that zone, the plate is non-compliant.
3. **The plate must stay a flat, unbroken rectangle.** The mark must be "exhibited on a flat rectangular plate or on a flat unbroken rectangular surface forming part of the motor vehicle" [3]. Cutting the plate into an L-shape, a curve, or any other shape fails this.
4. **The trim must not make the number harder to read.** You must not alter the plate so that characters become "less easily distinguishable" [3]. Cutting away the background border that gives the characters their contrast counts.

## So when IS a trim okay?

If the plate is cut down **with the characters still 70 mm tall, the 10 mm / 20 mm margins intact, and the shape still a flat rectangle** — the format rules are met. The law prescribes character sizes, spacing and minimum margins; it does not prescribe the overall plate size [3]. That is exactly why custom plates in Singapore are made to measure — to fit the recess while staying compliant.

If the trim touches the characters or the margins — don't. Hoping nobody notices is not a compliance strategy.
