Selling your car but want to keep the registration number? That's VRN retention — and done in the wrong order it quietly costs $1,200 extra. Here's the official process, fees and the traps, from LTA's own pages and forms.

## What retention is

Instead of bidding, "you can also choose to retain the VRN of your existing vehicle and assign it to your new vehicle" [4]. You apply before the old car is sold or de-registered; the number goes into a pool with your name on it until you're ready [11].

## The real cost: $100 — if you do it right

The fee structure confuses people, so here it is plainly:

- "Upfront fee to retain your VRN from a vehicle before transfer or deregistration Part 1: $1,300 (inclusive of GST)" [11].
- "If this VRN is used to register a new vehicle under your name, we will refund you $1,200 (inclusive of GST)" [11] — so the effective cost of carrying the number to a brand-new car is $100.
- If instead you fit it to an existing car you already own, no refund applies [11]; and if your system-generated number was auto-assigned first, "the retention fee payable is $1,300 (i.e. registered owner will need to pay an additional $1,200 if vehicle number was retained at $100)" [12].

## The 12-month clock

"Once your application to retain your VRN is approved, you have 12 months to use it before it expires" [11]. Extensions exist, but "there is no refund of the extension fee, or pro-rating of the extension period or fee" [11]. Plan the COE/car-changing timeline around the approval date, not after it.

## The $1,200 trap: tell your dealer

This is where owners lose money. The retained number "must be retained or bid in the applicant's name before registration" [12], and you must tell the dealer to use it: "ensure that the retained VRN is reflected accordingly to be used for the new vehicle registration. Otherwise, LTA will automatically assign a vehicle number to your new vehicle. Once a vehicle is assigned a vehicle number, it will not be considered a new vehicle, and you will not receive the refund of $1,200 (inclusive of GST)" [11]. Check the registration details the dealer shows you before confirming — the number on the paperwork must be yours.

## Then the 3-day plate rule kicks in

The number is only half the job. "Once you have replaced the VRN, you must change the number plates within 3 calendar days" [4], and standard registration requires you to "affix the registration number plates on your vehicle within 3 days of registration" [1]. On sealed schemes (ROPC, classic, vintage), "change and reseal the new vehicle number plates at any LTA-authorised inspection centre within 3 calendar days upon the successful retention of the vehicle registration number" [13].

## Quick decision table

- New car coming, same owner, want your number? Retain before selling old car — $100 effective cost [11].
- Keeping an existing car but want the number on it? Retain, then replace online — full $1,300 applies [11].
- Already registered with an auto-assigned number? Replacing it costs $327 plus the retention route [4].
