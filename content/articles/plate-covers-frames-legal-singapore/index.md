Carbon wraps, gel covers, chrome frames, "anti-cam" sprays — plate styling is a big business in Singapore. Some of it is fine, and some of it is a specific, written offence. Here's where the line sits in the Road Traffic rules.

## The wrapping itself: generally fine

Covering your car changes nothing in law by itself [3]. The rules regulate the *identification mark* — the plate and its surroundings — not the paint or vinyl elsewhere on the car [3]. Wrap away, as long as the plate area keeps meeting the rules below.

## Frame and bracket rule: nothing may crowd the number

"No other figures or letters and no design advertisement or ornamentation shall be placed near the identification mark in such a manner as to be liable to render it more difficult to read or distinguish" [3]. In practice:

- A plain frame that holds the plate flat and clear of the characters: fine under the obstruction rule [3].
- A frame with brand logos, slogans or a lip that covers part of the black surface or the margin: the margin rules (10 mm top/bottom, 20 mm sides) exist precisely so nothing crowds the characters [3].
- A "quick-release" or dangling frame is not securely fastened and flat, so it fails [3].

## Covers and sprays: this is the illegal one

The law was written with exactly these products in mind [3]. Rule 19A makes it an offence to alter any part of the plate surface so as to [3]:

- smoked or yellowed gel covers breach "cause the characters of the identification mark to be less easily distinguishable" [3];
- reflective sprays and films that flash back at cameras breach "cause the characters … to become or act as retroreflective characters" [3];
- number-plate "rain covers", prismatic tints and flip devices fall under the catch-all "prevent or impair the making of a true and clear photographic or recorded image of the identification mark" [3].

Driving a vehicle knowing its plate display has been tampered with this way is a separate offence [3]. And remember the rear plate must be "adequately illuminated" at night so every character is readable [3] — a dark cover breaks that too.

## Colour-shifting and "3D gel" styling

Re-colouring the characters themselves is not lawful: standard private cars use black-on-white front and black-on-yellow rear [1], and the character dimensions are fixed at 70 mm height with a 10 mm stroke [3]. A gel kit that bulges characters outside the official width, or glosses white characters into a different colour, fails the format rules no matter how neat it looks [3].
