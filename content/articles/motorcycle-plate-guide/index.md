Motorcycle plates have their own rulebook — smaller letters, different positions, special diagrams. Ride with the wrong plate and you're an offence waiting at the next checkpoint. Here's what the Road Traffic rules actually require.

## Motorcycles have their own plate diagrams

The law sets one format for cars and a dedicated set for two-wheelers: "in the case of motor cycles, the identification mark shall conform to Diagrams 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9" of the Fourth Schedule to the Road Traffic (Motor Vehicles, Registration and Licensing) Rules [3]. Those diagrams exist because a bike's mudguard simply cannot carry a car-size 70 mm plate.

## Size: smaller, but still exact

Car plates use 70 mm characters [3]. For motorcycles the law recognises two relief cases:

- **Front plate on the mudguard:** because it follows "the slight curvature of the mudguard", the standard character dimensions "may be halved" [3].
- **Rear plate:** motorcycles may substitute 50 mm high characters (with a 10 mm stroke) under the rear-plate substitution in the same Schedule [3].

Smaller is allowed — *messier* is not. The number still has to be vertical, complete and "easily distinguishable" [3].

## Position and height

A front motorcycle plate must sit "not more than 1.5 metres from the ground (when the motor cycle is unladen) in a vertical position", or, if it's a duplicate-face plate per Diagram 5, it must be fixed so the characters are readable from at least one face on either side [3]. That's the legal basis for the double-sided side-number plates popular on café racers and superbikes — they're compliant when they follow the diagram [3].

## Light, clean and unobstructed

The rear identification mark must be "adequately illuminated" between sunset and sunrise so every character stays readable [3]. Nothing may be placed near the mark "in such a manner as to be liable to render it more difficult to read or distinguish" [3] — that covers tail-tidy number brackets that shadow the plate, and tinted covers. And under rule 19A you must not alter the plate so characters become less distinguishable or act as retroreflective characters [3].

## Which prefix is yours?

Motorcycles have carried reserved prefixes from the very earliest series, when S'A was set aside for motorbikes [2], through the A and F series used for modern registrations [2].
